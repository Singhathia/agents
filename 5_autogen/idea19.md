Consider the current model for ultra-luxury: Sotheby's doesn't verify provenance by storing a central database. It curates through **trusted relationships with specialist dealers, families, and institutions.** The verification is *relational and reputational.*

Your solution—cryptographic provenance chains—is correct for *fungible verification* (art authentication, wine provenance). But for experiential curation (access to a private chef, an off-books Burgundian domaine, a dinner with a Renaissance scholar), the verification remains **relational.**

**The actual network architecture should be:**

1. **For asset-based services** (rare wine, art, collectibles): Cryptographic provenance chains + third-party authentication (Christie's, Sotheby's, certified sommeliers). This is the "verified scarcity" layer.

2. **For experiential services** (dining, travel, cultural access): A **curated network of premium providers** who are themselves verified through background, reputation, and ongoing audit. The AI doesn't verify their exclusivity; it verifies their *authenticity* and *availability.*

3. **For brokered services** (introductions, investments, acquisitions): **Third-party fiduciaries handle the introduction.** The AI curates the opportunity; lawyers, tax advisors, and family office staff execute the relationship.

This is messier than a purely algorithmic verification layer, but it's more aligned with how UHNW decision-making actually operates.
